# 7. Scrum Events

## 7.1 Introduction

Scrum Events are structured, time-boxed meetings that provide rhythm and consistency throughout the Scrum framework. These events create regular opportunities for planning, collaboration, inspection, and adaptation. Rather than being ordinary meetings, Scrum Events are designed to ensure transparency, improve communication, and continuously align the team's work with the project's objectives.

Every Sprint contains five official Scrum Events:

- Sprint
- Sprint Planning
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective

Each event serves a specific purpose and contributes to the successful delivery of valuable product increments.

---

# 7.2 Overview of Scrum Events

The Scrum framework follows a repeating cycle of events.

```
Sprint Planning
        │
        ▼
      Sprint
        │
        ▼
   Daily Scrum
        │
        ▼
  Product Increment
        │
        ▼
  Sprint Review
        │
        ▼
Sprint Retrospective
        │
        ▼
   Next Sprint
```

This cycle repeats until the product reaches its intended goals.

---

# 7.3 Sprint

## 7.3.1 Definition

A Sprint is the core event in Scrum. It is a fixed-length development cycle during which the Scrum Team works to achieve a specific Sprint Goal and produce a usable Product Increment.

A Sprint typically lasts:

- 1 week
- 2 weeks
- 3 weeks
- Up to 4 weeks

The duration remains consistent throughout the project to establish a predictable working rhythm.

---

## 7.3.2 Objectives of a Sprint

Each Sprint aims to:

- Deliver working software
- Achieve the Sprint Goal
- Produce customer value
- Reduce project risk
- Gather stakeholder feedback

Every Sprint ends with a potentially releasable Product Increment.

---

## 7.3.3 Characteristics of a Sprint

A Sprint has several important characteristics:

- Fixed duration (time-boxed)
- Clear Sprint Goal
- No changes that threaten the Sprint Goal
- Continuous collaboration
- Daily inspection
- Incremental delivery

These characteristics promote focus and predictability.

---

## 7.3.4 Benefits of Sprints

Working in Sprints provides several advantages:

- Faster delivery
- Early customer feedback
- Better planning
- Reduced uncertainty
- Continuous learning
- Improved quality
- Lower project risk

---

# 7.4 Sprint Planning

## 7.4.1 Definition

Sprint Planning marks the beginning of every Sprint.

During this event, the Scrum Team determines:

- What work will be completed.
- How the work will be accomplished.

The meeting is collaborative and involves the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers.

---

## 7.4.2 Objectives

Sprint Planning focuses on answering three key questions:

1. Why is this Sprint valuable?
2. What can be completed during this Sprint?
3. How will the selected work be completed?

The answers establish a shared understanding before development begins.

---

## 7.4.3 Inputs

Sprint Planning uses several important inputs:

- Product Backlog
- Product Goal
- Team capacity
- Previous Sprint results

---

## 7.4.4 Outputs

The primary outputs include:

- Sprint Goal
- Sprint Backlog
- Initial implementation plan

These outputs guide the team's work during the Sprint.

---

# 7.5 Daily Scrum

## 7.5.1 Definition

The Daily Scrum is a short meeting held every working day during the Sprint.

It is limited to **15 minutes** and enables Developers to synchronize their work.

The meeting is conducted at the same time and place each day whenever possible.

---

## 7.5.2 Purpose

The Daily Scrum helps Developers:

- Review Sprint progress.
- Coordinate daily work.
- Identify blockers.
- Update plans.
- Stay aligned with the Sprint Goal.

The meeting improves communication while reducing unnecessary discussions.

---

## 7.5.3 Typical Discussion

Although not mandatory, many teams answer three questions:

- What did I complete yesterday?
- What will I work on today?
- Are there any obstacles preventing progress?

These questions provide a quick overview of Sprint progress.

---

## 7.5.4 Benefits

Daily Scrums improve:

- Team communication
- Transparency
- Collaboration
- Early problem detection
- Sprint predictability

---

# 7.6 Sprint Review

## 7.6.1 Definition

The Sprint Review takes place at the end of each Sprint.

Its purpose is to inspect the completed Product Increment and gather stakeholder feedback.

Rather than serving as a formal acceptance meeting, it encourages open discussion about the product and future priorities.

---

## 7.6.2 Participants

Participants typically include:

- Product Owner
- Scrum Master
- Developers
- Customers
- Stakeholders

This broad participation ensures valuable feedback from multiple perspectives.

---

## 7.6.3 Activities

Typical Sprint Review activities include:

- Demonstrating completed features.
- Discussing achieved Sprint Goals.
- Reviewing Product Backlog progress.
- Gathering customer feedback.
- Updating future priorities.

---

## 7.6.4 Outcomes

The Sprint Review often results in:

- Updated Product Backlog
- New feature requests
- Changed priorities
- Improved customer understanding

---

# 7.7 Sprint Retrospective

## 7.7.1 Definition

The Sprint Retrospective is the final event of the Sprint.

Unlike the Sprint Review, which focuses on the product, the Retrospective focuses on the team's way of working.

Its objective is continuous improvement.

---

## 7.7.2 Objectives

The team evaluates:

- What went well?
- What problems occurred?
- What improvements should be made?
- How can collaboration improve?

The discussion is constructive rather than assigning blame.

---

## 7.7.3 Typical Topics

Teams often examine:

- Communication
- Teamwork
- Development practices
- Testing
- Sprint planning accuracy
- Process improvements

---

## 7.7.4 Outcomes

Common outcomes include:

- Process improvements
- Better collaboration
- Action items for the next Sprint
- Increased team efficiency

These improvements are applied immediately during the following Sprint.

---

# 7.8 Time-Boxing in Scrum

Every Scrum Event has a maximum duration.

| Event | Typical Duration |
|--------|------------------|
| Sprint | 1–4 weeks |
| Sprint Planning | Up to 8 hours (for a 1-month Sprint) |
| Daily Scrum | 15 minutes |
| Sprint Review | Up to 4 hours |
| Sprint Retrospective | Up to 3 hours |

Time-boxing prevents unnecessary meetings while maintaining focus and productivity.

---

# 7.9 Relationship Between Scrum Events

Each Scrum Event supports the next, creating a continuous improvement cycle.

| Event | Primary Purpose |
|--------|-----------------|
| Sprint Planning | Plan the work |
| Sprint | Build the product |
| Daily Scrum | Coordinate progress |
| Sprint Review | Inspect the product |
| Sprint Retrospective | Improve the process |

Together, these events ensure regular feedback, transparency, and adaptability.

---

# 7.10 Benefits of Scrum Events

Organizations benefit from Scrum Events through:

- Better communication
- Improved planning
- Continuous customer involvement
- Faster issue identification
- Higher product quality
- Stronger collaboration
- Continuous learning
- Greater adaptability

Because these events occur regularly, teams remain aligned with project objectives and customer expectations.

---

# 7.11 Practical Example

Imagine a team developing a mobile banking application.

- During **Sprint Planning**, the team selects user authentication features.
- Throughout the Sprint, Developers meet every morning for the **Daily Scrum**.
- At the end of the Sprint, stakeholders test the authentication system during the **Sprint Review**.
- Finally, the team discusses communication improvements during the **Sprint Retrospective** before starting the next Sprint.

This repeating cycle enables continuous product improvement while maintaining customer involvement.
