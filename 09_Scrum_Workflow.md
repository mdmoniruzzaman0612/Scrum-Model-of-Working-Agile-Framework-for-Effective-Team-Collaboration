# 9. Scrum Workflow

## 9.1 Introduction

The Scrum Workflow describes the sequence of activities through which a Scrum Team transforms customer requirements into valuable product increments. Unlike traditional software development models, where work progresses through fixed sequential phases, Scrum follows an **iterative and incremental workflow**. Each iteration, known as a **Sprint**, delivers a usable product increment while allowing continuous customer feedback and process improvement.

The workflow combines Scrum Roles, Events, and Artifacts into a continuous cycle that enables teams to deliver value rapidly while remaining flexible to changing business needs.

---

# 9.2 Overview of the Scrum Workflow

The Scrum workflow begins with customer or stakeholder requirements and ends with the delivery of a working product increment.

```
Stakeholder Requirements
          │
          ▼
   Product Backlog
          │
          ▼
   Sprint Planning
          │
          ▼
     Sprint Backlog
          │
          ▼
 Sprint Development
          │
          ▼
    Daily Scrum
          │
          ▼
 Product Increment
          │
          ▼
   Sprint Review
          │
          ▼
Sprint Retrospective
          │
          ▼
 Product Backlog Updated
          │
          ▼
      Next Sprint
```

Each Sprint repeats this cycle, gradually moving the product toward completion.

---

# 9.3 Step 1 – Product Backlog Creation

The workflow begins with the Product Backlog.

The Product Owner collaborates with customers and stakeholders to identify product requirements and convert them into Product Backlog Items (PBIs).

Examples include:

- User authentication
- Dashboard
- Payment integration
- Notifications
- Reporting features

Each item is prioritized according to its business value.

---

# 9.4 Step 2 – Sprint Planning

At the beginning of every Sprint, the Scrum Team conducts a Sprint Planning meeting.

During this meeting, the team:

- Reviews Product Backlog priorities.
- Selects work for the Sprint.
- Defines the Sprint Goal.
- Creates the Sprint Backlog.
- Estimates the work required.

The Sprint Planning meeting ensures that every team member understands the objectives before development begins.

---

# 9.5 Step 3 – Sprint Execution

Once Sprint Planning is complete, Developers begin implementing the selected backlog items.

Activities during Sprint execution may include:

- System design
- Software development
- Testing
- Database implementation
- Documentation
- Code reviews
- Integration

Throughout the Sprint, Developers collaborate continuously to achieve the Sprint Goal.

---

# 9.6 Step 4 – Daily Scrum

Every working day, the Developers participate in the Daily Scrum.

The meeting lasts no longer than **15 minutes** and serves to:

- Synchronize activities.
- Review Sprint progress.
- Identify obstacles.
- Adjust daily plans.
- Improve collaboration.

Rather than reporting to management, Developers coordinate directly with one another.

---

# 9.7 Step 5 – Product Increment

By the end of the Sprint, Developers produce a Product Increment.

The Increment must satisfy the team's Definition of Done, meaning it is:

- Functional
- Tested
- Integrated
- Potentially releasable

Every Sprint should produce measurable business value.

---

# 9.8 Step 6 – Sprint Review

The Sprint Review allows the Scrum Team to demonstrate the completed Increment to stakeholders.

Activities include:

- Product demonstration
- Customer feedback
- Requirement clarification
- Product Backlog updates
- Discussion of future priorities

The Sprint Review ensures that development remains aligned with customer expectations.

---

# 9.9 Step 7 – Sprint Retrospective

After the Sprint Review, the Scrum Team reflects on its own performance during the Sprint.

The Sprint Retrospective focuses on:

- Team collaboration
- Development practices
- Communication
- Quality improvements
- Process optimization

Rather than evaluating the product, the Retrospective evaluates the team's way of working.

The identified improvements become part of the next Sprint.

---

# 9.10 Continuous Improvement Cycle

One of Scrum's greatest strengths is its emphasis on continuous improvement.

Each Sprint generates:

- Customer feedback
- Technical learning
- Team experience
- Process improvements

These lessons influence future Product Backlog priorities and team practices.

This creates a self-improving development process.

```
Plan
   ↓
Develop
   ↓
Inspect
   ↓
Improve
   ↓
Repeat
```

---

# 9.11 Roles Within the Workflow

Every Scrum role contributes to the workflow.

| Role | Workflow Responsibility |
|------|--------------------------|
| Product Owner | Defines priorities and Product Backlog |
| Scrum Master | Facilitates the workflow and removes impediments |
| Developers | Build the Product Increment |

Because responsibilities are clearly defined, collaboration becomes more efficient.

---

# 9.12 Artifacts Within the Workflow

Artifacts guide the movement of work throughout the Sprint.

| Artifact | Purpose in Workflow |
|-----------|--------------------|
| Product Backlog | Source of future work |
| Sprint Backlog | Current Sprint work |
| Increment | Completed value delivered |

Artifacts ensure transparency at every stage of development.

---

# 9.13 Advantages of the Scrum Workflow

Compared with traditional development processes, the Scrum Workflow offers several advantages.

These include:

- Continuous customer involvement
- Frequent software delivery
- Rapid response to change
- Lower project risk
- Improved communication
- Better quality control
- Greater transparency
- Continuous learning

Because every Sprint ends with a working product increment, customers receive value much earlier than in traditional models.

---

# 9.14 Practical Example

Consider a team developing an online banking application.

### Sprint 1

- User registration
- Login system

↓

### Sprint 2

- Account dashboard
- Transaction history

↓

### Sprint 3

- Money transfer
- Notifications

↓

### Sprint 4

- Mobile optimization
- Security improvements

After every Sprint:

- Customers evaluate the product.
- Feedback is collected.
- The Product Backlog is updated.
- The next Sprint begins.

This iterative approach ensures that the product continuously evolves according to user needs.
