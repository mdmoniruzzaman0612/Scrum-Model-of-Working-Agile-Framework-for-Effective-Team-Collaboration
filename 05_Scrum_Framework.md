# 5. Scrum Framework

## 5.1 Introduction

The Scrum framework provides a simple yet highly effective structure for managing complex projects. Unlike traditional project management methodologies that rely on extensive planning and rigid processes, Scrum offers a lightweight framework that enables teams to deliver value incrementally while adapting to changing requirements.

The framework defines **who performs the work (Roles), how the work is organized (Artifacts), and when important activities occur (Events)**. Together, these components create a continuous cycle of planning, development, feedback, and improvement.

The Scrum Framework is intentionally minimal. Rather than prescribing detailed procedures, it establishes a set of responsibilities and practices that allow teams to organize themselves while maintaining transparency and accountability.

---

# 5.2 Overview of the Scrum Framework

The Scrum framework consists of three primary components:

- Scrum Roles
- Scrum Events
- Scrum Artifacts

These components work together to deliver valuable product increments during every Sprint.

```
Product Backlog
       │
       ▼
Sprint Planning
       │
       ▼
Sprint
       │
 ┌───────────────┐
 │ Daily Scrum   │
 └───────────────┘
       │
       ▼
Product Increment
       │
       ▼
Sprint Review
       │
       ▼
Sprint Retrospective
       │
       ▼
Next Sprint
```

The framework repeats continuously until the product reaches its desired goals.

---

# 5.3 Scrum Roles

Scrum defines three primary roles that collectively form the Scrum Team.

### Product Owner

Responsible for maximizing product value by managing and prioritizing the Product Backlog.

---

### Scrum Master

Facilitates Scrum practices, removes obstacles, and ensures that Scrum principles are followed.

---

### Developers (Development Team)

Cross-functional professionals responsible for designing, building, testing, and delivering working product increments.

Together these roles collaborate closely throughout every Sprint.

---

# 5.4 Scrum Events

Scrum uses a series of time-boxed events to create a predictable development rhythm.

The five official Scrum events are:

| Event | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| Sprint | Development cycle |
| Sprint Planning | Plan Sprint work |
| Daily Scrum | Synchronize daily work |
| Sprint Review | Demonstrate completed work |
| Sprint Retrospective | Improve team performance |

These events encourage regular communication, inspection, and adaptation.

---

# 5.5 Scrum Artifacts

Artifacts provide transparency regarding both the work being performed and the product being developed.

The three official Scrum artifacts are:

| Artifact | Purpose |
|-----------|----------|
| Product Backlog | Complete list of desired product features |
| Sprint Backlog | Selected work for the current Sprint |
| Increment | Completed and usable product |

Artifacts help every stakeholder understand project status at any point in time.

---

# 5.6 Scrum Workflow

A Scrum project progresses through a repeating cycle called a Sprint.

Each Sprint follows the same sequence of activities.

### Step 1 — Product Backlog

The Product Owner maintains a prioritized list of work items.

↓

### Step 2 — Sprint Planning

The Scrum Team selects backlog items to complete during the Sprint.

↓

### Step 3 — Sprint Execution

Developers build the selected functionality.

↓

### Step 4 — Daily Scrum

The team meets daily for coordination.

↓

### Step 5 — Sprint Review

Completed work is presented to stakeholders.

↓

### Step 6 — Sprint Retrospective

The team evaluates performance and identifies improvements.

↓

### Repeat

The cycle begins again with the next Sprint.

---

# 5.7 Iterative Development

One of Scrum's defining characteristics is iterative development.

Instead of waiting months before delivering software, Scrum delivers a usable product increment at the end of every Sprint.

Benefits include:

- Faster customer feedback
- Earlier defect detection
- Continuous improvement
- Reduced project risk
- Frequent value delivery

Each Sprint builds upon the previous one until the complete product is developed.

---

# 5.8 Incremental Delivery

Scrum promotes incremental product development.

Rather than delivering one large final release, functionality is delivered in small increments.

Example:

| Sprint | Delivered Feature |
|----------|------------------|
| Sprint 1 | User Login |
| Sprint 2 | Dashboard |
| Sprint 3 | Reports |
| Sprint 4 | Notifications |
| Sprint 5 | Payment System |

Customers begin receiving value much earlier than with traditional development approaches.

---

# 5.9 Feedback Loop

Continuous feedback is one of Scrum's greatest strengths.

Feedback is collected from:

- Customers
- Stakeholders
- Product Owner
- Developers
- Scrum Master

This feedback influences future Product Backlog priorities and ensures the product evolves according to customer needs.

---

# 5.10 Transparency Within the Framework

Transparency is maintained through several mechanisms.

Examples include:

- Visible Scrum Board
- Product Backlog
- Sprint Backlog
- Burndown Chart
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint Reviews
- Definition of Done

Because everyone shares the same information, decision-making becomes faster and more effective.

---

# 5.11 Advantages of the Scrum Framework

Organizations adopting Scrum often experience:

- Faster product delivery
- Better communication
- Higher software quality
- Increased adaptability
- Greater customer involvement
- Lower project risk
- Improved team morale
- Continuous learning

These advantages have made Scrum the most widely adopted Agile framework worldwide.

---

# 5.12 Example of the Scrum Framework

Imagine a team developing an online shopping application.

1. The Product Owner prioritizes customer requirements.
2. During Sprint Planning, the team selects features for the Sprint.
3. Developers implement the selected functionality.
4. Daily Scrums track progress and resolve blockers.
5. At the Sprint Review, stakeholders evaluate the completed work.
6. During the Sprint Retrospective, the team discusses improvements for the next Sprint.

This cycle repeats until the application is complete.
